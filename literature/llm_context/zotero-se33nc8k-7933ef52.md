## Abstract or detected summary

A molecular dynamics study of the effects of salt on the conformational preferences of leucine enkephalin (LE) in water has been performed to outline a general approach for quantifying the effects of a cosolvent on a biomolecular equilibrium in solution using molecular simulation. The simulations of LE suggest that the peptide exists as a mixture of folded and unfolded conformations in pure water, and a potential of mean force calculation indicates the population of the folded form to be 53±3%. The addition of 2 M salt reduces the population of the folded form to 12±5%. A combination of the simulation data and Kirkwood−Buff theory is used to determine the preferential interactions of the cosolvent with the peptide and to reproduce the population changes indicated by the potential of mean force calculations.
